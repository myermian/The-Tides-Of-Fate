# Chapter 2.1: The Isle of Rot (Calix & The Orb of Life)

## 🌴 Environmental Hazard: Corroding Sludge

> 📜 This shrine contains the **Orb of Life**.
>
> While the **Orb of Life** is corrupted, at the start of every combat round, all players take **1d4 Necrotic Damage** and healing is only half as effective; however, healing from **Cora's Sea-Salve** is not affected by this.
>
> Cleansing the **Orb of Life** provides a universal blessing, `The Orb of Life Blessing`, which provides **1d4 health regeneration** to all party members at the beginning of each round in combat.

## 💬 Arrival

The sea turns murky green as you close in on a jagged island of dying mangroves and black sludge. The air is thick with the stench of sulfur and rot, stinging your eyes and coating the back of your throat with a sharp, acrid taste. **Captain Tobias** presses a damp rag over his mouth and nose, his weathered face grim behind the cloth.

*"Breathe through your sleeves, lads. This place is pure rot. I'm dropping you off at the rock ledge, then pulling back to open waters before this muck eats through my ship's hull. Signal me when the air clears and I'll pick you up."*

The moment your boots hit the blackened rock, the smell hits you in full — a wall of putrid, chemical decay that makes your stomach clench. The island stretches out ahead of you in a tangle of sickly mangroves and gray, lifeless earth. The soil is slick and iridescent, glistening with a film of black bile that seems to pulse with a slow, toxic breath.

You make your way toward the shrine at the center of what was once a beautiful, pristine lake fed by a clear river. But the lake is beyond recognition. Where clear water once lapped gently against the shore, a thick, bubbling sludge now churns in sickly shades of green and black. It hisses where it meets the stone, and the wooden docks have warped and blackened, their planks soft and crumbling at the touch. The boats that once rocked in the current sit sunk and half-dissolved in the acidic murk, their hulls split open and weeping.

Standing at the entrance of the shrine is **Calix The Flesh-Rotter**. He is a towering brute clad in rusted plate mail that weeps black, acidic sludge from every seam, the metal pitted and groaning under its own corruption. His eyes burn with a feverish, relentless rage, and each labored step he takes sends a fresh trickle of black bile pooling at his feet.

*"Pain... everything is pain!"* he roars, his voice a ragged, broken thing, cracking like old timber. *"You will feel the pain that I feel."*

Without another word, he turns and stumbles into the shrine. The heavy stone doors grind shut behind him with a final, earth-shaking thud, sealing his anguish — and the **Orb of Life** — inside.

> 📜 The party is now stranded on the island, facing a toxic lake that blocks direct access to the shrine. They must explore the surrounding terrain to find another way in.
>
> 💡 To encourage the party to explore the island rather than attempting to swim across the lake, they should initially take minor damage if they attempt to swim through. If this happens, warn them that continuing to stay in the lake will accelerate the damage and lead to death.

### 💬 The Toxic Lake

Before the party can gather their thoughts, a boar bursts from the mangroves, bristles raised and eyes wild. It is being hunted by a **Corpse-Sludge Crawler**. It runs in pursuit of the boar, jaws snapping, a high and reedy shriek tearing from its maw.

The boar's headlong sprint carries it toward the corroded docks. Its hooves, heavy and panicked, give way and it slips. Then the boards break, and the boar plunges into the sludge. The party has a clear, unobstructed view of what happens next. For one heartbeat, the boar thrashes with a brief, desperate convulsion. Then the sludge hisses, and the creature begins to dissolve from the inside out. Black bile bubbles up in thick, oily columns around its flanks. The bones, or what's left of them, float to the surface for a moment before sinking and vanishing into the churning murk. The lake glistens where the boar fell. The stench lingers, sharp and chemical, and the water where it dissolved is a shade darker than the rest.

The **Corpse-Sludge Crawler** halts at the edge of the docks. Its shriek cuts off. It turns, its jaws parting to reveal row upon row of needle-thin, rotting teeth, and locks onto the party.

> 📜 This opening is meant to demonstrate is the lake is not merely a barrier but an active, corrosive hazard which will cause death.
>
> 🌴 If the party attempts to swim across the lake, they take **1d6 + 4 Acid Damage** at the start of each round they remain in the water. After each round, damage increases by another **1d6 Acid Damage** per round with no ceiling. Describe the burning, the stinging, the slow erosion of skin and armor. Warn them clearly that staying in the water will kill them.
>
> 💡 The party could ask about the distance to the shrine at the center of the lake, and if the party planned well enough, they could push through the damage and possibly brute force their way to the shrine, albeit heavily damaged. One possibility is that they could take a running start, jump as far into the lake as possible and swim across the rest of the way.
>
> 💡 Another creative possibility is if anyone has any equipment or spell that allows them to float or fly, they could use that to get across the lake.

## 🗺️ Exploration

*Players are free to explore the island. The large mountains and deep forest make it difficult to observe where to head to first.*

> 📜 If the party possesses the notes from **Finn's Scouting Intel**, then describe the details that are normally acquired from reaching the top of the plateau, but on the map that **Finn** provided.

* **Shore:** To your south is where you landed.
* **Plateau:** To the west of the lake there is a plateau with steep cliffs overlooking the entire island.
* **Deep Forest:** To the east of the lake there is a deep forest with a river feeding into the lake.
* **Forest Mountains:** To the north of the lake there are large mountains dusted with dense forests at their base.

### 📍 The Shore

You head back to shore hoping to find some clues as to what to do, but there doesn't seem to be anything useful. **The Salty Sea-Dog** is completely out of sight, eliminating the possibility of asking for aid.

### 📍 The Plateau

You head to the plateau and are standing in front of a very steep, narrow trail carved into the cliff face. The stone is slick with the island's toxic residue, handholds are loose and crumbling, and a misstep means a long drop back to the shore below. Staring at the steep cliff face, you are a bit uneasy. Do you climb to the top or do you turn around and try to find another path?

> 📜 If the party decides to climb to the top, each player must succeed on three separate **DC 14 STR (Athletics) checks** to make the climb to the top. A failed check means the player slips, takes **1d6 bludgeoning damage**, and must retry. A party member can use their **Help Action** to grant advantage to a climber. Climbing down afterwards does not require any checks.
>
> The climb is intentionally difficult but not lethal. The Plateau is a risk and the intel gained here is valuable, but the cost is time and a small amount of health.
>
> 💡 The party could be creative to send only a single party member to make the climb so that they are not all required to do an Athletics check.

At the top, the wind picks up and the stench thins to a distant murmur. The entire island spreads out below, and three things stand out:

* **The Waterfall:** To the north, a curtain of dark, oily water tumbles off the mountain face. The source of the river that feeds the lake seems to come from the mountains. The current is steady and powerful.
* **The Cabin:** Nestled at the base of the waterfall, half-hidden by twisted trees, is a small stone cabin. Moored beside it at the river's edge is a boat with a dark, seamless hull that carries a faint, shifting iridescence. The sludge swirls around it without clinging. It looks unaffected by the toxicity of the sludge.
* **The Deep Forest:** To the east, the river winds in a long, serpentine path through the forest before emptying into the lake. The banks are narrow and overgrown.

> 📜 If the party has **Finn's Scouting Intel**, this confirms what they already know. Without it, this is the first visual evidence that a solution to cross the lake safely exists.

### 📍 The Deep Forest

The deep forest to the east is a wall of black-barked trees, their canopies so thick that the light filtering through is green and sickly. The ground is soft, spongy, and coated in a film of black spore. The air is heavy, still, and dead. There are no birds, no insects, just the slow drip of toxic moisture from the leaves.

The river runs through the heart of the forest, its banks narrow and overgrown. The path following the river upstream is treacherous. The trail is half-swallowed by roots, the ground is slick with rot, and the river itself is a constant, hissing presence at the party's heels.

> 📜 The Deep Forest is the most dangerous walking route to the mountains. The river winds in long, serpentine curves, and the path is poorly defined. It should feel claustrophobic and hostile. Following the river is the only way forward, but it is a hostile environment in itself. As the party progresses upstream, the creatures grow bolder and the path narrows. The contrast when they finally emerge into the open ridge of the **Forest Mountains** should feel like surfacing from deep water.
>
> 🌴 The toxic river is active. Creatures in the water will attack the party as they navigate the banks.
>
> 💡 Use your best judgement to determine how many random battles the party faces on their way to the the **Forest Mountains** until they reach the stone cabin.

### 📍 The Forest Mountains

The mountains to the north are shrouded in a low mist that smells of wet stone and decay. The trail up is overgrown, the path choked with black-veined vines and dead undergrowth. The toxic film on the ground is thicker here, and the air is heavy.

As the party crests the ridge, it is right there: a small, weathered stone cabin tucked against the base of the mountain.

Moored at the river's edge beside the cabin is the boat made of the enchanted wood (TODO: name of enchanted wood). Its hull is dark and seamless, with a faint, shifting iridescence that catches the light like oil on water. Where the toxic river laps against its sides, the water recoils, hissing softly, unable to cling. The boat is intact, dry, and waiting.

> 📜 This is the convergence point. Regardless of which route the party took — the Plateau, the Deep Forest, or a direct approach through the mountains, the enchanted boat is the only means of crossing the lake. Once the party has the boat, they can ride it down the river.
>
> If the party attempts to open the cabin door, there is a creature residing inside that will attack them.
> 
> 🌴 While riding the boat down the river, the party will encounter hostile creatures in the water. These creatures are smaller, faster, and attacking in numbers. The boat's hull protects the party from the water itself, but the creatures are a separate threat.
>
> 💡 The party can be creative and choose to carry the boat down to the lake instead. The boat is lighter than normal and requires a combined **DC 40 STR (Athletics) check** to maintain their grip and footing. If the combined check fails, the boat is dropped and alerts the creature residing inside the stone cabin to burst through the door and attack.
>
> 💡 The party can choose to explore the stone cabin. If they do they will face bile creature residing within unless he has already burst out and attacked. If they have not faced the creature, indicate that upon opening the door the creature charges at them and attacks them.

#### 💬 The Cabin

The party enters the cabin The stench of rot is thick, but beneath it lingers a faint, clean scent of dried herbs and ink. It seems that this cabin had someone living inside. A writing desk sits against the far wall, buried under scrolls and half-finished notes. A single dead oil lamp guards a leather journal, its final page still open. The shelves hold rows of bottled reagents and anatomical sketches, and in the corner a workbench carries the remnants of meticulous, patient brewing.

> 📜 The former resident is assumed dead as there is an unfinished journal, a ruined workbench, and a creature that laid claim to the residence all point to the person having been claimed by the corruption.
>
> 👤 **The Scholar of the Isle:** The journal belongs to a wandering scholar who came to the island to study **Celestia** and her origins. Fascinated by the divine light she carried, the scholar spent years capturing the light eminating from the orb into a single, sealed **Vial of Celestial Water**, and brewed a stock of three **Potion of Greater Healing**s for his own protection. He never meant to stay; the corruption took the island first. His final entry, long before the party's arrival, reads: *"The light I have bottled is no gift for the world. I thought of using it against the creature stalking me, but I believe it is too important to use it on such a lowly creature. If it ever must be spent, let it be spent against the true rot that devoured this place that seems to have corrupted the shrine."*
>
> The party can explore the island, read the journal, and take the **Vial of Celestial Water** and **Potion of Greater Healing**s. There are no additional items or lore to discover.
> * **Vial of Celestial Water** *(Wonderous Item, Unique)*: A small glass vial of luminous, slow-turning liquid that glows with a warm, inner light, distilled from the divine light of **Celestia**. The vial is a single use item and can do massive radiant damage when used does **8d8 Radiant Damage**.
> * **Potions of Greater Healing** — each restores **4d4 + 4 hit points** when drunk.
>
> The Vial is the island's answer to the sludge and is very potent against it. Spending it on **Calix** is a deliberate, hard-earned reward for the party's exploration, so let it land with weight: the radiant burst should visibly scorch the rot, hiss the black bile away, and drive him back. It is strong enough to turn the tide of the boss fight, but it is not a guaranteed kill.
>
> 💡 The cabin is an optional detour, not a forced encounter. Reward the party for taking the time to clear the study and read the journal, but do not block the lake crossing on it. If they already fought the cabin creature (for example, it burst through the door when they dropped the boat), present the interior as already cleared and let them collect the loot.

## 💬 Riding The Boat

> 📜 This section could potentially be skipped if the party was creative enough to decide to carry the boat down the mountain path. If it is skipped, proceed directly to **The Shrine: The Orb of Life**.

The enchanted boat slides off the river's edge and catches the current with a low, resonant hum. The moment it is underway, the calm gives way. The water churns into a series of steep rapids, each drop a fresh plunge that throws the little craft sideways against the banks and twists the current into cold, black whirlpools. The stench of the river rises off the water in hot, acrid waves, and the party can feel the hull shudder under the strain of the fall.

Halfway down the river, the current suddenly stills. The water widens into a short, black stretch, and from its depths rises a great, slow heave. Something enormous is moving beneath the surface, its back breaking the water in broad, glistening ridges of bile and bone. It turns toward the boat with a low, wet growl that rattles in the chest, and the enchanted wood beneath the party hums in a low, warning note.

> 📜 This is a short, fast-paced mini game built around a **DC 13 DEX (Acrobatics)** check for each party member. The checks represent the crew wrestling the tiller, bracing against the rapids, and steering the enchanted boat as it barrels down the river toward the creature.
>
> **Everyone Succeeds:** The crew works the tiller in perfect unison. The enchanted boat heels hard across the current, threading a narrow gap between the creature's jaws and the bank. The beast lunges, its maw snapping shut on empty water, and the boat rockets past it.
>
> **At Least Half Succeed (but not everyone):** The crew loses some control, but the enchanted boat still has momentum. Instead of steering clear, the party drives the bow straight into the creature. The impact is catastrophic. The beast is crushed beneath the enchanted hull and dies on the spot. As it dies, it erupts in a burst of black, acidic sludge that spatters across the whole party; each party member must make a **DC 14 DEX Saving Throw** or take **3d6 Acid Damage** (half on success).
>
>**More Than Half Fail:** The boat is wrenched off course, and the creature jumps onboard. The enchanted wood hisses and recoils at the bile. The party must fight it on board. When it is finally reduced to **0 HP**, it bursts in a geyser of black sludge; every creature within **10 feet** must make a **DC 14 DEX Saving Throw** or take **3d6 Acid Damage** (half on success).
>
> 🌴 The enchanted hull protects the party from the river itself, but the creature and its death-burst are separate threats. The burst is a key reminder for the party: even a clean victory on this island comes at a cost.
>
> 💡 Gauge how many checks each party member makes based on your judgement: the number of rapids you want to narrate, the party's level, and the current difficulty tier. A larger or more skilled party may benefit from fewer checks per player so the result still feels like a genuine test rather than a foregone conclusion; a struggling party may benefit from an extra check to swing the outcome.

## 💬 The Shrine: The Orb of Life

The enchanted boat shudders to a halt as the current gentles and the river finally opens out onto the great, sickly lake. Beyond the blackened docks and the half-dissolved hulls of a dozen dead boats, the shrine stands at the center of the lake. A squat, ancient structure of dark stone, its walls weeping the same black bile that coats the rest of the island. Its doors are the heavy stone slabs that **Calix** sealed behind himself when he fled inside, and they have not moved since.

The party takes a moment to catch their breath, then approaches. The doors are cold to the touch, and faintly warm in places, as if something inside is still burning. When they are finally pried and pulled apart, they groan open on rusted hinges, and the stench from within is tenfold worse.

Inside, the shrine is a single, vaulted chamber. The **Orb of Life** floats at its center, pulsing with a dull, sickly green light that has long since curdled. And before it, his back turned, stands **Calix The Flesh-Rotter**. He turns, his feverish eyes locking onto the party, and he raises his rotting battle axe with a ragged, broken snarl.

*"You want the pain?"* he roars. *"Then feel it with me."*

## 💬 The Cleansing

**Calix** staggers to his knees, his rotting battle axe slipping from his grip and clattering across the stone. The black sludge that has wept from his armor slows, then stills, and for the first time the feverish rage in his eyes fades into something exhausted, almost relieved. He looks at the **Orb of Life**, then back at the party, and his voice is almost gentle.

*"The pain... it is finally quiet."*

He slumps, and the rusted armor that has carried him so long seems to ease, the black bile drying to a dull, lifeless crust. The corruption that bound him to the orb is broken. A black void appears behind him and he falls into it while it closes behind.

The **Orb of Life** shudders. The sickly green at its core flickers, gutters, and then blooms into a warm, radiant bright light that warms the chamber. The light spills through the open doors and across the blackened stone, and where it touches, the island begins to heal. The black bile across the shrine walls dries and flakes off, revealing clean, pale stone beneath. Beyond the doors, the party can see it spreading. The churning sludge of the great lake calming, the blackened docks lightening, and the sickly mangroves straightening as fresh, green life creeps back into their leaves. The stench of sulfur and rot, which has clung to the back of their throats since they landed, finally thins and lifts into the clean, salt-tinged air.

The **Orb of Life** settles into a steady, green pulse at the center of the shrine, and its light wraps gently around the party, warm and steady. A new strength settles into their limbs, a quiet, constant warmth that will follow them for the rest of the journey.

> 📜 The **Orb of Life** is now cleansed. The negative localized effect on this island is removed, and the island is completely safe (no environmental hazards, no combat) for any future revisit. The party now carries `The Orb of Life Blessing`, which provides **1d6 health regeneration** to all party members at the beginning of each round.

Having seen the flash of bright light emanating from the shrine, **Captain Tobias** takes this as the signal. He returns back to the island to pick them up before they set sail on their next destination.
